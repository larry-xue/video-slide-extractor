# Slide-Detection Method Benchmark

Compares three ways of turning a slide-bearing video into captured slides:

| Method | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed interval | Screenshot every 10 s — what most tutorials do |
| Naive per-pixel diff | A pixel "changed" if its mean \|ΔRGB\| > 25; new slide when > 2% of pixels changed |
| Block diff (this package) | 8×8-block mean absolute difference, defaults and a tuned `changedRatio` |

Results: **[RESULTS.md](./RESULTS.md)** (regenerated by `run.mjs`, raw numbers in `results.json`).

## Ground truth by construction

Instead of hand-labeling a recording, the fixtures are *rendered from known
slide sequences*, so every transition timestamp is exact by construction and
the whole benchmark is reproducible from two scripts:

```bash
node bench/generate-fixtures.mjs   # renders the videos (needs ffmpeg)
node bench/run.mjs                 # runs the methods, writes RESULTS.md
```

Two slide sources × three variants = six videos:

- **synthetic** — 30 slides drawn deterministically by ffmpeg (no external assets)
- **mit** — the 46 slides Video2Any extracted from MIT OCW 6.0001 Lecture 1
  (© MIT OpenCourseWare, [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/),
  [source lecture](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-0001-introduction-to-computer-science-and-programming-in-python-fall-2016/resources/lecture-1-what-is-computation/)),
  fetched at run time from [the public deck page](https://video2any.com/decks/mit-what-is-computation) — not committed to this repository

and per source:

- **clean** — 960×540, hard cuts, crf 23
- **noisy** — heavy compression + down/upscale (codec noise, blur)
- **overlay** — clean + an animated "webcam" box bottom-right (presenter overlay)

Slide durations are drawn from a seeded PRNG (4–12 s, seed `60001`), so every
machine renders byte-identical timelines. The three variants of a source share
one timeline, isolating the *content degradation* variable.

Note the mit fixture measures detection on a **re-rendering built from
extracted stills** — hard cuts at known times — not on the original lecture
recording. Real recordings add crossfades, camera motion, and in-slide builds;
see the failure-class list in [docs/evaluation.md](../docs/evaluation.md) and
the (separate, manually-labeled) product benchmark protocol in
[awesome-video-to-slides](https://github.com/larry-xue/awesome-video-to-slides).

## Protocol

Follows [docs/evaluation.md](../docs/evaluation.md):

- Sampling frozen for all methods: one 160×90 RGBA frame every 2 s via FFmpeg.
- Detections matched 1:1 to ground-truth transitions within ±2.5 s.
- Reported per video: precision, recall, F1, slide coverage (slides with ≥ 1
  capture), duplicate rate (extra captures of an already-captured slide).
- Environment (Node, FFmpeg, package commit) recorded in `results.json`.

What this benchmark deliberately does **not** measure: duplicate collapse
across revisited slides, clean-frame selection, cropping/masking, OCR, or
export quality — those are converter pipeline stages, not change detection.
